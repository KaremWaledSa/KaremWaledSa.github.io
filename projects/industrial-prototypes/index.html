PROJECT CONTEXT

Physical prototypes · Professional work

01 / ENGINEERING PROBLEM

What the system needed to do.

Turn separate mechanical, electrical, and control elements into working educational and industrial demonstrators that make system behavior visible.

02 / ENGINEERING APPROACH

How the work comes together.

Assembled and integrated a process-training station combining tanks, valves, pumps, sensors, and PLC-based sequencing.

Worked on a DC-motor linear-motion trainer, connecting the mechanical transmission, motor drive, and movement constraints.

Supported prototype design, assembly, and integration of Raspberry Pi mobile robots and interactive robotic-arm systems.

03 / RESULT & SCOPE

What the work demonstrates.

Four physical devices documented with authentic photographs and demonstration videos. The collection shows practical R&D, assembly, and troubleshooting across multiple engineering disciplines.

04 / KAREM WALED — RESPONSIBILITY

What I owned and contributed.

  • Participated in the design and execution of all four devices.
  • Led practical R&D, assembly, and subsystem integration work.
  • Connected mechanical, electrical, sensing, control, and computing elements into demonstrable systems.
  • Supported iteration and troubleshooting when behavior at one subsystem affected another.

05 / SYSTEM BREAKDOWN

How the system is structured.

  • PLC process station: tanks, pipework, pumps, valves, sensors, and a control cabinet.
  • DC motor trainer: motor drive, mechanical transmission, guided travel, and movement constraints.
  • Mobile robots: Raspberry Pi compute, motor control, chassis, power, and sensing.
  • Interactive robots: mechanisms, actuation, embedded control, and programmed demonstrations.

06 / TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

Engineering decisions and execution.

01

Prototype integration

Each device required mechanical fit, electrical continuity, correct power distribution, sensor placement, actuator behavior, and control logic to agree at the same time.

02

R&D iteration

Development involved assembling a testable version, observing system behavior, isolating the responsible subsystem, and refining the design or integration.

03

Training-oriented design

The systems were built so learners and operators could see the relationship between a command, an electrical response, and the resulting physical behavior.
